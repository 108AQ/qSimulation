## 1. Sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalisation (SKQD) — **CORRECTED (title); not published in a journal**

- **Title (exact):** "Quantum-Centric Algorithm for Sample-Based Krylov Diagonalization" (not "Sample-based Krylov quantum diagonalisation"; the acronym SKQD is used inside the paper).
- **Authors (20):** Jeffery Yu, Javier Robledo Moreno, Joseph T. Iosue, Luke Bertels, Daniel Claudino, Bryce Fuller, Peter Groszkowski, Travis S. Humble, Petar Jurcevic, William Kirby, Thomas A. Maier, Mario Motta, Bibek Pokharel, Alireza Seif, Amir Shehata, Kevin J. Sung, Minh C. Tran, Vinay Tripathi, Antonio Mezzacapo, Kunal Sharma.
- **arXiv:** 2501.09702 (v1 16 Jan 2025; v3 17 Sep 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09702 . Comments: 22 pages, 6 figures.
- **Journal:** none. The arXiv record carries no journal reference or DOI; a Crossref title search returns nothing; papers from 2026 still cite it as arXiv:2501.09702. Cite as a preprint.
- **Algorithm:** Krylov states |psi_k> = exp(-i k H dt)|psi_0>, k = 0..d-1, generated with the **full Hamiltonian H** (Trotterised on hardware); each Krylov state is sampled in the computational basis; the subspace is the span of all distinct bitstrings sampled across the d Krylov states (after self-consistent configuration recovery, U(1) symmetry, as in SQD; plus a "carry-over" of basis states with |Psi_j| > tau = 1e-8 between recovery iterations, used only in the 4-impurity experiments); H is projected onto that subspace and diagonalised classically (Frontier).
- **Convergence guarantee (Theorem 1, main text and SI Sec. II):** Let the ground state |phi_0> be (alpha_L, beta_L)-sparse in the computational basis (the L largest-amplitude bitstrings carry total weight >= alpha_L and each has |c_j|^2 >= beta_L), and let |Psi> be the lowest-energy state supported on those L bitstrings. Then
  `<Psi|H|Psi> - E_0 <= sqrt(8) ||H|| (1 - sqrt(alpha_L))^{1/2}`, provided all L important bitstrings are sampled; all L are sampled with probability >= 1 - eta as long as the number of samples **from each Krylov state** exceeds `d^2 log(L/eta) / ( |gamma_0|^2 (beta_L - 2 sqrt(eps~)) )`, where |gamma_0|^2 = |<psi_0|phi_0>|^2 is the reference-state/ground-state overlap and eps~ = 2 - 2 sqrt(1 - eps/Delta E_1) with eps the Krylov (KQD) energy error.
  - Time step: dt = pi / Delta E_{N-1} (Delta E_{N-1} = spectral width), the choice of Epperly-Lin-Nakatsukasa; the KQD energy error (SI Theorem 2 = Epperly et al. Thm 3.1) decays exponentially in the Krylov dimension d at a rate set by the gap-to-bandwidth ratio Delta E_1 / Delta E_{N-1}, so d = O(log(1/eps)) for a "well-behaved" spectrum (Delta E_{N-1} not growing too fast, Delta E_1 not too small).
  - Overlap: the proof needs only **polynomial overlap**, |gamma_0|^2 in O(1/poly(n)) (main text: "when the initial state has polynomial overlap"); constant-error statements are phrased with |gamma_0| = Theta(1).
  - Sparsity: L = O(poly(n)). Under these assumptions the algorithm "converges in polynomial time" (samples scale as d^2 / |gamma_0|^2 per Krylov state). Note the error bound is controlled by the ground-state weight *outside* the L sampled bitstrings, not directly by the overlap.
- **Demonstrations:** single-impurity Anderson model (SIAM) with 41 bath sites (42 electrons in 42 spinful orbitals) using **85 qubits** (84 system + 1 auxiliary) on **ibm_fez** (Heron r2, 156 qubits), circuits with up to 6153 two-qubit gates, 1e5 shots per Krylov dimension; also 29 bath sites on 61 qubits. A 4-impurity model with 7 bath sites per impurity (28 bath sites, 32 electrons in 32 orbitals): 64 system qubits in a **70-qubit** circuit (the extra qubits are auxiliary connectors between the spin-up and spin-down chains), ~6e3 two-qubit gates (613 per Trotter step). Classical diagonalisation on the Frontier supercomputer. Results agree with DMRG and HCI.

## 2. Sample-based quantum diagonalisation (SQD) — **VERIFIED**

- Javier Robledo-Moreno, Mario Motta, Holger Haas, Ali Javadi-Abhari, Petar Jurcevic, William Kirby, Simon Martiel, Kunal Sharma, Sandeep Sharma, Tomonori Shirakawa, Iskandar Sitdikov, Rong-Yang Sun, Kevin J. Sung, Maika Takita, Minh C. Tran, Seiji Yunoki, Antonio Mezzacapo, "Chemistry beyond the scale of exact diagonalization on a quantum-centric supercomputer", **Science Advances 11 (25), eadu9991 (20 June 2025)**, DOI 10.1126/sciadv.adu9991; arXiv:2405.05068 (v1 8 May 2024, v3 13 Jul 2025), https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05068 .
- **Key result:** N2 dissociation in cc-pVDZ with a (10e, 26o) active space (58 qubits incl. auxiliary; 52 system qubits, 1792 two-qubit gates), [2Fe-2S] (30e, 20o) in TZP-DKH (45 qubits; 40 system, 1100 two-qubit gates) and [4Fe-4S] (54e, 36o) (77 qubits; 72 system, 3590 two-qubit gates, 10,570 total gates) on the 133-qubit Heron processor **ibm_torino** (ibm_nazca for an N2/6-31G test), with classical diagonalisation on up to 6,400 Fugaku nodes (subspace dimension up to 1e8, K = 10-100 batches). The circuit is a truncated local unitary cluster-Jastrow (LUCJ) ansatz with CCSD-derived parameters (no variational optimisation on hardware); sampled bitstrings define subspaces whose Davidson eigenvalues are variational upper bounds. Accuracy: for N2/cc-pVDZ the SQD energies are "everywhere within tens of mEh" of the HCI reference (so not chemical accuracy); [2Fe-2S] and [4Fe-4S] are assessed by energy-variance extrapolation against DMRG; the paper does not claim chemical accuracy for hardware results.
- **"Configuration recovery"** = *self-consistent configuration recovery*: because the circuit conserves particle number (and S_z), sampled bitstrings with the wrong number of spin-up/down electrons are known to be corrupted; each such bitstring is repaired by flipping |N_x - N| bits chosen probabilistically with weights given by the distance between each bit value and the average orbital occupations n_{p sigma} estimated from the previous iteration's eigenvector (initially from the symmetry-preserving raw samples); the loop (sample -> repair -> diagonalise -> update occupations) is iterated until convergence (typically 3-10 iterations).

## 4. QCELS — **VERIFIED (journal title as given)**

- Zhiyan Ding and Lin Lin, "Even Shorter Quantum Circuit for Phase Estimation on Early Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computers with Applications to Ground-State Energy Estimation", **PRX Quantum 4, 020331 (2023)** (published 26 May 2023), DOI 10.1103/PRXQuantum.4.020331; arXiv:2211.11973 (v2 10 Mar 2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11973 .
- **Precise claims (from the paper):** Let p_0 = |<psi_0|psi_ground>|^2. (Thm 1/2) If **p_0 > 0.71**, choose delta = Theta(sqrt(1 - p_0)); multi-level QCELS estimates lambda_0 to precision eps with probability >= 1 - eta using maximal evolution time **T_max = delta/eps** (so the depth prefactor delta -> 0 as p_0 -> 1), total evolution time **T_total = Theta~(delta^{-(1+o(1))} eps^{-1})** (Heisenberg-limited), and N N_s = Theta~(delta^{-(2+o(1))}) circuit repetitions (N time points x N_s shots per point). (Thm 3/Cor. 4) For small p_0 the method is combined with the Lin-Tong Fourier filter: with a spectral gap Delta = lambda_1 - lambda_0 >> eps and knowledge of the relative overlap p_r(I, I') >= 0.71, T_max = Theta~(Delta^{-1}) + delta/eps and T_total = Theta~(p_0^{-2} delta^{-(2+o(1))} Delta^{-1} + ...), i.e. provably reduced depth whenever the *relative* overlap (overlap conditioned on a window) is large even if p_0 is small. Numerics: circuit depth reduced by about two orders of magnitude relative to the Lin-Tong method, even at p_0 = 0.6.

## 5. Lin and Tong 2022 — **VERIFIED**

- Lin Lin and Yu Tong, "Heisenberg-Limited Ground-State Energy Estimation for Early Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computers", **PRX Quantum 3, 010318 (2022)** (published 2 Feb 2022), DOI 10.1103/PRXQuantum.3.010318; arXiv:2102.11340, https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11340 .
- **Key scaling (Corollary 3):** with access to controlled exp(-i tau H) and **one ancilla qubit**, and given a known lower bound eta <= p_0 on the overlap of the initial state with the ground state, the ground-state energy is estimated to additive error eps with maximal evolution time (circuit depth) **O~(eps^{-1} polylog(eps^{-1} tau^{-1} eta^{-1}))** and total evolution time **O~(eps^{-1} eta^{-2})**, using M = O(eta^{-2} log^2(d) ...) Hadamard-test samples at random times; **no spectral-gap assumption** is needed (the method inverts an approximate cumulative distribution function of the spectral measure). The paper itself notes the eta^{-2} dependence is far from the optimal eta^{-1/2}.

## 9. Theory of quantum subspace diagonalisation — **both VERIFIED (Kirby arXiv ID supplied)**

- Ethan N. Epperly, Lin Lin, Yuji Nakatsukasa, "A Theory of Quantum Subspace Diagonalization", **SIAM J. Matrix Anal. Appl. 43 (3), 1263-1290 (2022)**, DOI 10.1137/21M145954X; arXiv:2110.07492 (v2 13 Jun 2023).
  - **Key statements:** the noisy generalised eigenproblem (H~, S~) is solved by *thresholding* (canonical orthogonalisation): discard eigenvalues of S~ at or below a threshold eps, project (H~, S~) onto the retained eigenvectors, take the smallest eigenvalue. Informal Theorem 1.2 / Theorem 2.7: if the perturbations of H and S have norm <= eta and the least eigenvalue of the thresholded pair is well separated, then with threshold eps = Theta(eta^{1/(1+alpha)}) (alpha in [0, 1/2], empirically ~1/4) the recovered least eigenvalue (its arctangent) has error O(d_0^{-1} eta^{1/(1+alpha)}) where d_0^{-1} is its condition number (so error ~ eta^{4/5} x condition number). Theorem 3.1 (Krylov approximation + thresholding, time grid t_j = pi j / Delta E_M): 0 <= E~_0 - E_0 <= [ (1 + pi Delta E_1/Delta E_{N-1})^{-2k} ... + 2 Delta E_{N-1} eps_total ] / ( |gamma_0|^2 - 2|gamma_0| sqrt((2k+1) eps) ), i.e. exponential convergence in the number of time steps at rate set by the gap Delta E_1 relative to the bandwidth, with thresholding costing only a 1 + O(sqrt(k eps)) prefactor plus a term proportional to the discarded S-eigenvalue mass; Remark 3.2: the initial overlap |gamma_0|^2 must be reasonably large (e.g. >= 0.1), unlike classical Krylov. The analysis also explains why "fake" small eigenvalues appear without thresholding.
- William Kirby, "Analysis of quantum Krylov algorithms with errors", **Quantum 8, 1457 (29 Aug 2024)**, DOI 10.22331/q-2024-08-29-1457; **arXiv:2401.01246** (v7 26 Aug 2024), https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01246 . Key result: non-asymptotic upper and lower bounds on the ground-state energy from real-time quantum Krylov algorithms with generic errors in the circuit outputs; the upper-bound error is **linear** in the input error rate (resolving the earlier provable 2/3-power scaling), via an effective target Hamiltonian in an effective Krylov space.

## 18. PMSV and Bayesian QPE with error detection — **CORRECTED (PMSV reference) / VERIFIED (Bayesian QPE)**

- **PMSV (partition-measurement symmetry verification)** was introduced in: Kentaro Yamamoto, David Zsolt Manrique, Irfan T. Khan, Hideaki Sawada, David Munoz Ramo, "Quantum hardware calculations of periodic systems with partition-measurement symmetry verification: Simplified models of hydrogen chain and iron crystals", **Phys. Rev. Research 4, 033110 (2022)** (9 Aug 2022), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.4.033110; arXiv:2109.08401. So "Phys. Rev. Research 4, 033110" is right but the author list is Yamamoto, Manrique, Khan, Sawada, Munoz Ramo (Cambridge Quantum Computing + Nippon Steel), not Yamamoto/Duffield/Kikuchi/Munoz Ramo. Key idea: Z2/U(1) symmetries written as Pauli strings are appended to each measurement circuit of a commuting partition of Hamiltonian terms and measured alongside it; shots violating the symmetry are discarded, at no extra two-qubit-gate cost. The hardware in that paper was **ibmq_casablanca** (IBM Falcon), 2-qubit iron models within ~5 kJ/mol of state-vector results.
- **Bayesian QPE with error detection:** Kentaro Yamamoto, Samuel Duffield, Yuta Kikuchi, David Munoz Ramo, "Demonstrating Bayesian quantum phase estimation with quantum error detection", **Phys. Rev. Research 6, 013221 (2024)** (29 Feb 2024), DOI 10.1103/PhysRevResearch.6.013221; arXiv:2306.16608. Device and result: **Quantinuum H1-1** trapped-ion computer; the 3-qubit QPE circuit for a 2-qubit H2 Hamiltonian (plus a dummy qubit) is encoded in the [[6,4,2]] Iceberg code (6 code qubits + 2 ancillas = 8 physical qubits); Bayesian (adaptive) phase estimation with optimal parameter selection; circuits with up to **920 physical two-qubit gates**, ground-state energy within **6e-3 hartree** of exact. This paper does not use PMSV.

## 21. Ethylene torsional barrier — **VERIFIED (experimental); CAS(2,2) number NOT FOUND in print**

- **Experimental:** John E. Douglas, B. S. Rabinovitch, F. S. Looney, "Kinetics of the Thermal Cis-Trans Isomerization of Dideuteroethylene", **J. Chem. Phys. 23 (2), 315-323 (1955)**, DOI 10.1063/1.1741959. From the thermal cis-trans isomerisation of CHD=CHD at 450-550 C they obtain an activation energy / torsional barrier of **65 kcal/mol** (as quoted, with this attribution, in Zen et al. 2014 below). A spectroscopic re-evaluation of the torsional levels gives a lower value, ~60 kcal/mol: R. Wallace, "The torsional energy levels of ethylene: a re-evaluation", Chem. Phys. Lett. 159 (1), 35-36 (1989), DOI 10.1016/S0009-2614(89)87449-4. (Background review: A. J. Merer and R. S. Mulliken, "Ultraviolet spectra and excited states of ethylene and its alkyl derivatives", Chem. Rev. 69, 639-656 (1969), DOI 10.1021/cr60261a003.) No NIST tabulation of this barrier was found.
- **Computed reference values** (secondary source that tabulates them: A. Zen, E. Coccia, Y. Luo, S. Sorella, L. Guidoni, "Static and Dynamical Correlation in Diradical Molecules by Quantum Monte Carlo Using the Jastrow Antisymmetrized Geminal Power Ansatz", J. Chem. Theory Comput. 10 (3), 1048-1061 (2014), DOI 10.1021/ct401008s, arXiv:1406.4330, Table III): RHF 108.4; UHF 49.6; CCSD 89.9; CASSCF(12,12)/cc-pVTZ 69.1; MR-CISD+Q 69.2; VMC/JAGP 71.9(1); LRDMC/JAGP 70.2(2) kcal/mol; "quantum chemistry methods provide estimations of the barrier height within 62-73 kcal/mol". Other quoted values: CASSCF(12,12)/cc-pVDZ 68.2 and CASPT2 65.5 kcal/mol (cited in Coe, Taylor, Paterson, arXiv:1212.2526); CASPT2 65.2 kcal/mol (Lopez, Piris, Matxain, Ruiperez, Ugalde, ChemPhysChem 12, 1673 (2011), as cited in Chai, arXiv:1610.02018); a 2026 preprint (Vaish & Rubenstein, arXiv:2602.04783, Table 2) reports RHF 111.5, MP2 102.6, CCSD 90.7, CCSD(T) 79.3, CIPSI 75.0 kcal/mol for its own (rigid-rotation) setup — note that the geometry treatment (relaxed vs rigid) differs between studies and shifts the number by several kcal/mol.
- **CAS(2e,2o):** I could not find a paper that prints a CASSCF(2,2) barrier number for ethylene. The closest: Saade & Burton, "Excited state-specific CASSCF theory for the torsion of ethylene", arXiv:2402.13046 (2024) uses a minimal (2e,2o) active space but tabulates excitation energies, not the ground-state barrier; Wang et al., "Quantum computation of conical intersections on a programmable superconducting quantum processor", arXiv:2402.12708 (2024) uses CAS(2e,2o) ethylene at 90 degrees torsion. What can be stated with sources: CASSCF without dynamic correlation overestimates the barrier (CASSCF(12,12) 68-69 vs CASPT2 65.2-65.5 vs experiment 65), so a (2e,2o) model should be expected above the experimental value; any specific CAS(2,2) number must be computed or taken from a source not located here (PySCF is not installed in aq-env, so no check was run).
